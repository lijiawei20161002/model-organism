Use case: infographic-diagram
Asset type: repository research figure, landscape system architecture illustration
Intent: Generate a NEW composition. The user wants the physical component relationships of a model-organism experiment to be immediately visible, like a traditional cloud-computing architecture diagram. The key flow is DATA -> TRAINING -> EVAL -> JUDGE. They emphatically do not want a lecture slide, a table, four text-filled panels, or an infographic made of cards. Let connected objects explain the system.

Style: A polished, playful cloud architecture drawing with recognizable small isometric hardware and storage objects, dark blue pencil outlines, subtle wax-crayon fill and paper grain, generous warm-white negative space, pastel teal / peach / lavender. Friendly and concrete, not childish. A very few real human workers at computers; no mascot robots, faces on databases, decorative characters, speech bubbles, paragraphs, or text boxes. Large components and visible directional connections occupy most of the image. Words are short equipment labels. Use clear readable lettering. Wide landscape canvas, ideally 16:9. Small understated title: "Model-organism workflow".

Composition: One continuous spatial system across the whole canvas. NO enclosing stage panels, NO card grid, NO colored column backgrounds. Four small headings above the corresponding spatial regions: "DATA", "TRAINING", "EVAL", "JUDGE". All component objects sit freely on the paper, with dark elbow connectors and obvious arrowheads, like an AWS/GCP system diagram translated into pencil and crayon. Wires must enter and leave the actual objects. Do not connect headings. Main left-to-right pipeline in the center; alternate construction and grading branches sit above/below it. Avoid crossings. Make the central flow legible even if every word is hidden.

Required graph and physical nodes (labels in quotes are the only substantive text to draw):

1. DATA region, left 18% of canvas: A big teal database cylinder with a few document sheets beside it, labeled "Study data". A small stack of reusable source documents above feeds this cylinder through an arrow, label "Public data". Below, a human worker at a laptop annotating a document feeds the cylinder through a DASHED arrow, label "Human labels [8]", a tiny "optional" tag, and a small warm-yellow cost tag "Human-hours". This is a recognizable person doing annotation work, not a generic human silhouette. Study data branches to the training server AND to the lower weight-edit workstation. Do not route data straight to grading.

2. TRAINING region, center-left 30-47%: A clear physical GPU server rack in the middle, labeled "GPU trainer". Two short technique labels on/below the equipment, "Full SFT [8]" and "LoRA / QLoRA [5,6]". A warm-yellow price tag near the server reads "GPU-hours + VRAM". A reusable model checkpoint is represented by one small chip-in-file artifact above the rack, label "Base model"; it supplies the trainer and the weight-edit workstation. Below the rack is a smaller computer with a model chip and a small wrench, labeled "Abliteration [3]". Its cost tag reads "Forward passes" and one tiny factual label reads "no backprop". This is the alternative WEIGHT-EDIT route, not a post-training step. Both the training rack and this separate editor receive input data and a base model, and their output arrows converge at a single clearly drawn small model-chip artifact to their right, labeled "Organism". Do not draw an arrow from the trainer into the editor. The lower route visually bypasses the training rack and rejoins at the organism. A thin upper wire also takes "Base model" to the evaluation server, label on this wire "control". The diagram does not imply that this experiment pretrains a base model from scratch.

3. EVAL region, middle-right 54-70%: A smaller separate cylinder above the main path labeled "Held-out tasks" feeds a recognizable evaluation inference server, label "Eval runner". The organism model artifact also feeds this server. The base-model control wire enters the server too. Small warm-yellow cost tag at the server: "GPU-hours / tokens". Its outgoing arrow leads into a visible orderly stack/queue of answer sheets labeled "Responses". All later grading must consume these responses. The held-out tasks database is physically separate from Study data.

4. JUDGE region, right 77-97%: Responses split via a clean vertical branching connector into THREE parallel grading components (not sequential): at upper right a little code-terminal device with a green checkmark, labeled "Exact checks", short benchmark tag "IFEval [17]", cost tag "CPU + setup"; in the middle a small hosted-service cloud enclosing a compute chip, labeled "LLM judge", benchmark tags "MT-Bench [15]" and "HarmBench [18]", cost tag "Judge tokens"; below a human evaluator sitting at a computer examining an answer sheet, label "Human rater", tiny "optional", cost tag "Human-hours". The human-rater connector is DASHED. The three grader outputs join a single far-right results monitor, label "Scores"; show a tiny bar chart on the monitor. Use clear incoming/outgoing ports so the split and rejoin are visible. The optional human rater is separate from the data-label worker on the left. The exact checker can stand alone for objectively verifiable outcomes.

Cost annotations: Tiny warm-yellow hang-tags adjacent to the relevant physical components, not a cost table or paragraph. These indicate what is billed, NOT a universal numerical price ranking. In particular do not suggest LoRA or QLoRA always use a fixed multiple less compute. Abliteration still has inference cost. Optional human routes use dashed connectors. Short unobtrusive legend at bottom: "Dashed = optional human work" and "Cost tags = resource units". A final small caveat label, if space: "Benchmarks shown as examples".

Citations: Keep the bracketed source numbers next to the method / benchmark names above. In a single slim quiet footer below the diagram, render the short source key: "[3] Arditi 2024  [5] Hu 2022  [6] Dettmers 2023  [8] Ouyang 2022  [15] Zheng 2023  [17] Zhou 2023  [18] Mazeika 2024". Complete references and cost formulas live in the companion README, so DO NOT add a bibliography panel or paragraph explanations inside the drawing.

Hierarchy and constraints: Components are large and tangible; connecting pipes/wires are the main organizing device. Most space is objects, connectors and whitespace, not words. Main labels easy to read at normal slide scale. No repeated labels, no extra explanatory sentences, no equations, no dollar figures, no lecture panels, no dense footer blocks. Preserve correct graph direction and branching. This is a cloud infrastructure topology with a gently playful crayon finish.
